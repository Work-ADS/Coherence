# AFI Digital Solutions — Brand v1

**Locked:** 2026-06-25
**Owner:** Digital Solutions Department (design team)
**Scope:** House brand for the DS team — design system, demos, internal showcases. Wealth Planner 2026 is the first showcase; retirement, home energy and future simulators will ride the same brand and the same system.

**Out of scope:**
- Client deployments (Santander, Kutxabank, Unicaja, Renta4, Mutualidad, etc.) — those re-skin onto the client's own design system.

## 4. Personality (5 traits)

### 1. Confident, not loud
*Why:* Wealth planning is the most trust-sensitive software a client will ever see. Anything that shouts for attention reads as a sales pitch — and clients with real money tune out sales pitches in two seconds. Confidence is the *absence* of effort; the brand doesn't prove itself with chrome.
*How:* Bold type, restrained color, generous whitespace. No neon. No motion for motion's sake.

### 2. Storytelling
*Why:* The advisor is walking the client through 30 years of compound returns, tax scenarios, retirement triggers, legacy structures — a wall of numbers no human can absorb at a glance. If the UI doesn't pre-digest it, the client checks out and the meeting stalls. A headline collapses 20 numbers into one sentence the client can hold onto.
*How:* Outcome first ("cuando jubiles, tendrás 5 millones"), data second. Every screen has a sentence; every chart has a thesis.

### 3. Modern, made to age
*Why:* Money conversations are stressful — retirement, inheritance, what-if-I-die scenarios. Modernity does two jobs at once: it signals competence (the bank invested in this; they take it seriously) *and* it injects small moments of delight into a heavy topic, so the meeting doesn't feel like a notary appointment. Stripe-level polish lowers the anxiety floor. Simulating your future, especially when you have real money, should feel a little fun.
*How:* Editorial-grade craft, sharp typography, considered transitions. Built to read right in 2029 — no trends that age in 18 months.

### 4. Hands-on
*Why:* A wealth plan isn't a single answer; it's a conversation between scenarios. The meeting that breaks is the one where the client asks *"but what if I retire 3 years earlier?"* and the advisor says *"let me run that and get back to you."* Clients with real money *expect* to play with their plan. Flexibility is the difference between a planner and a calculator.
*How:* Every input is a control, not a form field. Charts respond live. Scenarios sit side by side. Nothing on screen feels final until the client decides.

### 5. Delightful, even when the topic is heavy
*Why:* Money conversations are stressful — retirement, legacy, what-if-I-die scenarios. The dominant feeling in the room is anxiety, and most planning software *doubles down* on it (more numbers, more warnings, more red). We do the opposite. Small, earned moments of delight ease the tension and make the client *excited* about the future they're planning toward. Delight isn't decoration; it's how we lower the anxiety floor enough for the client to actually plan. *Compatible with Trait 1 — this is **quiet** delight: Stripe's satisfying button press, not Robinhood's confetti.*
*How:* Tactile micro-interactions (scrubbing values, dragging scenarios, chart morphs). Earned celebration moments (hitting a goal, crossing a milestone, the optimistic scenario lighting up with a little more energy). Optimistic outcomes feel optimistic — color, motion, weight tell you when you're winning. Energy over solemnity, without sacrificing restraint.

## 5. Values (4 commitments)

### 1. Institutional brain, startup velocity
*Why:* Most fintech products move fast but lack deep topic expertise — they ship features that don't survive a real conversation with a regulator or a senior advisor. Most finance consultancies have the expertise but ship slowly, white-glove, no product mindset. AFI sits in the rare middle: a traditional finance house with decades of cross-market expertise (Spain, EU, USA), but the digital solutions team moves like a startup. Most fintechs don't have that domain depth across the *whole* org. That's the moat.
*How:* Every product decision is informed by real expertise — the people writing the copy could sit across from a CIO as peers. Defaults reflect real Spanish/EU/US wealth norms (not "approximated from Stack Overflow"). We ship in days what consultancies ship in quarters.

### 2. Easy to understand, even when complex
*Why:* The product handles wealth complexity so the user doesn't have to. If a screen needs a tooltip to be understood, the screen failed. If a chart needs a legend with 8 entries, it needs to become two charts. The bar is *"any HNW client can read it cold."*
*How:* Progressive disclosure (simple vs. avanzado toggles), plain-language labels, headline-led layouts. Information density only where it earns its keep.

### 3. Built from the surface up
*Why:* The DS isn't a top-down architecture — it grows from real demos. Every screen we ship surfaces a missing primitive, a token gap, a pattern ready to formalize. We over-engineer on purpose: tokens, semantic layers, primitives so cleanly defined that AI can spot the pattern differences between AFI and Santander just by reading the JSON. That rigor underneath is what buys the freedom on top — wild typography, new chart languages, modern design experiments — because the bones absorb the risk. Most teams trade velocity for consistency; we get both by being slightly overkill.
*How:* Tokens → semantics → primitives → patterns, every layer documented for humans AND for AI. Bespoke markup is forbidden where a primitive exists. Brand variants ship as token swaps, never forks. New visual directions ship as feature branches the system can absorb.

### 4. Demo-grade craft
*Why:* This brand exists to *sell* — internal showcases, sales pitches, conference talks. "It works" is a lower bar than "it lands in a meeting." Pixel discipline, motion polish, semantic clarity are all sales tools, not aesthetic indulgences.
*How:* Every interaction designed for a stranger watching for the first time. No half-finished states. Loading, empty, error states all polished — not just the happy path.

## 6. Visual Idea

**Story-led product design with a modern editorial sensibility.**

*Why:* The truth says decisions happen through stories. Editorial design (in its modern, web-native form) is the discipline that's spent the most time figuring out how to tell complex stories beautifully. Strip away the "old newspaper" connotation and what's left is: type doing heavy lifting, layouts that breathe, color carrying meaning, charts that read like sentences. That's the sensibility — not the magazine vibe.

*How it shows up (directional, not prescriptive):*
- **Typography:** Hierarchy is unmissable. Headlines feel like statements; numbers feel like numbers.
- **Color:** Carries meaning — outcomes, ranges, status. Restrained by default, vivid where it earns it.
- **Layout:** Generous canvas. Density only where it earns its keep.
- **Charts:** Title is the story; the chart is the proof. Hover, click, scrub — the chart answers questions.
- **Motion:** Considered. Transitions tell you what changed.
- **Earned, restrained delight:** The visual language deliberately looks for moments of energy — micro-interactions that feel tactile, transitions that reveal, milestone moments that animate just enough to feel like a win. Delight is restrained but felt; it's how the brand keeps serious topics from feeling solemn.

*Modern moodboard references:*
- **Stripe** — product polish, type-led marketing
- **Linear** — bold restraint, modern UI density done right
- **Vercel / Framer** — generous canvas, editorial-grade landing surfaces
- **NYT interactive features** — story-led data viz (COVID curves, election graphics — closest thing to "modern editorial finance" alive today)
- **The Pudding** — data journalism applied to product
- **Apollo / Notion / Things 3** — modern productivity polish
- **Wealthsimple / Robinhood** (cleanest screens) — modern consumer fintech that still feels premium
- **Apple product pages** — premium editorial sensibility applied to product

*Open territory:* glassmorphism, dark mode, gradients, motion-led storytelling — all on the table for v1 if they serve the story. Dashboard-of-widgets layouts are reserved for v2 (see Rollout below). The language emerges by building and iterating, not by gatekeeping.

## Rollout phases

### v1 (now) — Colors, fonts, surface
Visual refresh of existing flows. Typography, color, restrained delight micro-interactions, story-led headlines on charts. *No flow or experience changes.*

### v2 (future) — Structural
Flow rework. Reimagined experience. Layout direction freed up — including **dashboard-of-widgets** style if it serves the story. The over-engineered DS foundation (Value 3, "Built from the surface up") is what makes this swap cheap when we get there.
